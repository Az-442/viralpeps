Comparing listings

How to read a peptide listing before you buy

Peptides are usually advertised by total content alone, which is not enough to compare two products. The figure that matters is concentration.

A listing that says 20 mg tells you what is in the container, not how strong it is. Twenty milligrams in a 3 ml cartridge is 6.67 mg/ml; the same 20 mg in a 2 ml vial is 10 mg/ml. Both are honestly labelled and they are not the same product. Total content divided by volume gives the concentration, and that is the number worth comparing across suppliers.

It also determines cost. Price per milligram is a fairer comparison than headline price: a larger cartridge at a higher price is frequently cheaper per milligram than a small vial. Every product page here states total content, cartridge volume, the resulting concentration and how the dial is graduated, so the comparison can be made without inference.

The second thing to check is whether the concentration is fixed at manufacture or created by the buyer. A lyophilised vial has no concentration until it is reconstituted, so it depends on how much diluent is added and how carefully — which is why two researchers working from identical vials can end up with different material. A pre-mixed cartridge has one concentration from the moment it is filled.

What are research peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids joined by peptide bonds, the same building blocks that make up proteins. A research peptide is synthesised to a defined sequence for use in controlled laboratory work.

Product description

BPC-157 supplied as 5 mg of lyophilised powder in a sealed vial. A synthetic pentadecapeptide of fifteen residues, sequence GEPPPGKPADDAGLV, molecular weight 1419.5 g/mol.

What is BPC-157?

The name stands for Body Protection Compound. The sequence corresponds to a fifteen-residue partial sequence of a protein found in human gastric juice, and the reasoning behind its isolation is worth understanding: gastric mucosa sits in an extremely acidic environment and repairs itself unusually quickly. Researchers at Zagreb pursued the question of what in gastric juice might account for that, and synthesised this fragment.

It is not a naturally occurring peptide in its own right. It is a synthesised partial sequence, which is why the certificate of analysis confirms identity against a reference standard rather than against a biological source.

What the literature actually shows

Published work covers angiogenesis, tendon and ligament models, gastrointestinal mucosal models and peripheral nerve models. Hsieh and colleagues reported effects on VEGFR2 signalling in the Journal of Molecular Medicine in 2017. Chang and colleagues published tendon fibroblast work in the Journal of Applied Physiology in 2011. Sikiric and colleagues reviewed the compound in Current Medicinal Chemistry in 2012.

Two limitations should be stated plainly, because they bear on how results are interpreted. Almost all published work is in rodent models, with no completed human trials of consequence. And a large proportion of the literature originates from a single research group, which means independent replication is thinner than the volume of publications suggests.

BPC-157 and TB-500

These two are frequently mentioned together and are chemically unrelated. BPC-157 is a fifteen-residue synthetic sequence at 1419.5 g/mol. TB-500 is a seven-residue acetylated fragment of thymosin beta-4 at 889.0 g/mol. Different sequences, different masses, different mechanisms in the literature. Studying either in isolation gives interpretable results; studying a fixed-ratio blend of both does not, because an observed effect cannot be attributed to either component.

For that reason we supply both as single-compound vials rather than as a premixed blend.

Does it come with a certificate of analysis?

Every batch is supplied with a certificate of analysis stating the batch reference, identity confirmation, chromatographic purity against specification, and the analytical method used. Purity is determined by reverse-phase HPLC with mass spectrometric identity confirmation. The certificate is specific to the batch you receive rather than a generic sample document, and states the laboratory that performed the work.

How is it reconstituted?

Vials arrive as a lyophilised cake under vacuum. Store sealed at −20 °C, protected from light. Allow the vial to reach room temperature before breaking the seal, which prevents condensation drawing moisture into the powder.

Reconstitute with bacteriostatic water directed down the vial wall, not onto the cake. Swirl gently; do not shake or vortex. The 5 mg cake dissolves readily and the solution should be clear and colourless with no visible particulate.

Once in solution, BPC-157 should be kept refrigerated at 2 to 8 °C and protected from light. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which degrade peptides faster than steady refrigeration. Reconstituted material is not indefinitely stable and working solutions are best prepared in the volumes actually needed.

Cold chain and UK dispatch

Held at −20 °C from arrival through to dispatch. Orders ship in insulated packaging with a cold pack, tracked, so material spends as little time as possible at ambient temperature. Short transit excursions are normal for lyophilised peptides and do not compromise a sealed vial, but the powder should go back into cold storage on arrival.
